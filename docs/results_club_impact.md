# Part 4: what a shift in transfer valuation would do to club income and value

Generated by `src/09_club_impact.py`. Counterfactuals reprice each transfer in the fee model's sample (N = 34) by changing only the price of attention; performance, age, position and buyer stay fixed.

## Inputs

- Price of attention in fees: β_B = 0.324 (+25% per doubling, 95% CI +6% to +48%).
- Price of attention in market values: β_A = 0.170 (+12% per doubling). Fees price fame about twice as steeply as market values.
- Player sales ≈ 36% of operating revenue (UEFA: €10.3bn summer-2026 sales vs €28.6bn 2024 revenue).
- Club value ≈ revenue × a multiple (Forbes: most clubs below 4×, top-30 average 5.6×). A *permanent* change in income therefore moves club value by the same percentage; a *one-off* change moves it by that percentage divided by the multiple (4× used). Forbes excludes player trading from the revenue it values, so the permanent case is an upper bound.

## Scenarios

| Scenario | What changes | Total fees in sample |
|---|---|---|
| S1 performance-only pricing | fame stops affecting fees | −1% |
| S2 fees price fame like market values | fame priced at β_A instead of β_B | −1% |
| S3 fame premium grows by half | β_B × 1.5 | +4% |

Removing the fame premium barely changes the size of the market (−1% of total fees, about €0.1bn of UEFA's €10.3bn summer-2026 sales). It mainly **moves money between clubs**: clubs selling famous players receive less, clubs selling under-the-radar players receive more.
